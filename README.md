## An introduction to a priori power analysis through simulation

How many participants do we need for a study, and how can we justify this number? A priori power analysis is an essential tool for research planning, yet traditional analytical approaches can become difficult to apply when experimental designs, statistical models, or inferential goals become more complex.

In this presentation, we introduce simulation-based power analysis as a flexible and intuitive framework for sample-size planning. Using practical examples in `R`, we illustrate how statistical power influences the variability of research outcomes and how repeated simulations can be used to evaluate the probability of achieving a predefined inferential goal. We first consider conventional null-hypothesis significance testing before extending the approach to Bayesian inference, including decisions based on regions of practical equivalence (ROPEs) and estimation precision.

Finally, we discuss the importance of sensitivity analyses, realistic assumptions, and reproducible reporting. The overarching goal is to demonstrate that sample-size planning is not simply about achieving 80% power, but about designing studies capable of providing sufficiently informative answers to meaningful scientific questions.
